Data center tier ratings, explained

The Uptime Institute's four-tier classification defines how much redundancy a data center has, and how much downtime to expect.

Tier Redundancy Expected Uptime Typical Use
Tier I No redundancy 99.671% Small business, non-critical workloads
Tier II Partial redundancy (N+1) 99.741% Growing businesses with some criticality
Tier III Concurrently maintainable 99.982% Enterprise workloads requiring high availability
Tier IV Fully fault-tolerant (2N) 99.995% Mission-critical systems, no acceptable downtime

CoreGenix designs to the tier level that matches your actual business risk — not the highest tier by default, which often isn't cost-justified for every workload.

Two concepts worth understanding

Redundancy levels and storage types come up in every data center conversation — here's the short version of each.

N, N+1, and 2N Redundancy

N is the minimum capacity needed to run the facility, with no spare. N+1 adds one extra component beyond what's needed, so a single failure doesn't cause an outage. 2N fully duplicates the entire system for maximum resilience — the standard for Tier IV facilities.

SAN vs. NAS vs. Software-Defined Storage

  • SAN — block-level storage over a dedicated network, for databases and virtualization
  • NAS — file-level storage over standard networks, for shared file access
  • Software-Defined Storage — flexible storage management independent of hardware

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about data center design, tiers, and hosting models.

What are data center solutions?
Data center solutions cover the design, construction, and management of the facilities that host servers and critical applications — including power, cooling, racks, storage, and virtualization.
Should we build our own data center or use colocation?
It depends on budget, control needs, and scale. In-house data centers offer maximum control but require capital investment; colocation provides facility infrastructure without the capital cost; cloud eliminates physical infrastructure entirely. CoreGenix advises objectively on all three.
How much does a data center setup cost in India?
Data center costs vary widely with size, redundancy level, and whether it's a server room or full facility. CoreGenix offers a free data center assessment to scope requirements before providing a fixed quote.
What are data center tier ratings?
Data center tier ratings, defined by the Uptime Institute, classify facilities from Tier I to Tier IV based on redundancy and expected uptime. Tier I offers basic infrastructure with no redundancy, while Tier IV provides fully fault-tolerant infrastructure with 99.995% or higher expected uptime.
What is N+1 redundancy in a data center?
N+1 redundancy means having one more component than the minimum required to run the facility — for example, if three cooling units are needed, a fourth is added so any single unit can fail without affecting operations. 2N redundancy doubles the entire system for even higher resilience.
What is the difference between SAN, NAS, and software-defined storage?
SAN (Storage Area Network) provides block-level storage over a dedicated high-speed network, typically for databases and virtualization. NAS (Network Attached Storage) provides file-level storage accessible over standard networks, suited to file sharing. Software-defined storage abstracts storage management from the underlying hardware, offering flexibility across both models.
How long does a data center or server room build take?
A server room build-out typically takes a few weeks to a couple of months, while a full enterprise data center facility can take several months depending on scale, redundancy level, and civil work required.
