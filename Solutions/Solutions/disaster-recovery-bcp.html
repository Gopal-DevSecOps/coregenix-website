What is disaster recovery & business continuity planning?

Disaster recovery (DR) is the process of restoring IT systems and data after a disruption, while business continuity planning (BCP) covers how the wider business keeps operating during that disruption. CoreGenix delivers both together — DR strategy with defined RPO/RTO, replicated recovery architecture, tested failover through regular drills, and business continuity plans covering critical processes beyond IT.

The problem we solve

When disaster hits — fire, flood, cyber attack, power failure — most businesses are caught unprepared. Systems go down, data is at risk, and nobody has a tested plan to recover.

A DR plan that exists only as a document nobody has tested

No defined RPO or RTO — nobody knows how much data is at risk or how long recovery takes

Backup exists, but no replicated systems ready to actually fail over

IT recovery plans that ignore how the rest of the business keeps running

No clear playbook — the first hour of a disaster spent figuring out who does what

No board-ready documentation to satisfy auditors or insurers

Every hour of downtime costs money and reputation. The businesses that survive disruption are the ones that planned for it before it happened.

RPO vs. RTO, explained

The two numbers that actually define your recovery plan.

RPO — Recovery Point Objective

The maximum acceptable amount of data loss, measured in time. If your RPO is 4 hours, your last good backup or replica can be no more than 4 hours old when disaster strikes.

Answers: "How much data can we afford to lose?"

RTO — Recovery Time Objective

The maximum acceptable downtime before systems must be restored and operational again. If your RTO is 2 hours, operations must resume within 2 hours of an outage.

Answers: "How long can we afford to be down?"

Hot site vs. warm site vs. cold site

Three levels of recovery site readiness — the right one depends on your RTO and budget.

Fastest recovery

Hot Site

A fully replicated, ready-to-use environment that can take over almost instantly — highest cost, lowest RTO.

Balanced

Warm Site

Some infrastructure already in place, but final setup and data sync are needed before it's fully operational.

Lowest cost

Cold Site

Basic space and power with no pre-installed systems — lowest cost, but the longest time to activate.

DR vs. business continuity

Disaster recovery restores IT systems and data after a disruption — servers, applications, and infrastructure back online. Business continuity is broader: it covers how the entire business keeps functioning during that disruption, including manual workarounds, staff communication, supplier coordination, and customer service continuity — regardless of whether IT is fully restored yet. A DR plan without a BCP leaves the business unable to operate even after systems come back; a BCP without DR has no technical foundation to recover to. CoreGenix builds both together as one coordinated plan.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about DR, BCP, RPO/RTO, and recovery sites.

What is the difference between DR and BCP?
Disaster recovery (DR) restores IT systems and data after a disruption. Business continuity planning (BCP) covers how the wider business keeps operating during that disruption, including alternate processes, staff communication, and critical function continuity beyond just IT. Most businesses need both together.
What are RPO and RTO?
RPO (Recovery Point Objective) is the maximum acceptable amount of data loss, measured in time. RTO (Recovery Time Objective) is the maximum acceptable downtime before systems must be restored and operational again. CoreGenix defines both for each system based on business criticality.
How often should we test our DR plan?
DR plans should be tested at least annually, and after any major infrastructure change. Regular testing through scheduled drills is the only reliable way to know a DR plan will actually work when needed.
What is the difference between a hot site, warm site, and cold site?
A hot site is a fully replicated, ready-to-use environment that can take over almost instantly. A warm site has some infrastructure in place but needs final setup before use. A cold site is basic space and power with no pre-installed systems, requiring the most time to activate.
What is a Business Impact Analysis (BIA)?
A Business Impact Analysis identifies critical business functions, the systems they depend on, and the financial and operational impact of their disruption over time. It forms the foundation for setting RPO, RTO, and continuity priorities.
How much does disaster recovery planning cost in India?
DR and BCP costs depend on the number of systems, required recovery speed, and chosen recovery site type (hot, warm, or cold). CoreGenix offers a free DR and BCP assessment to scope requirements before providing a fixed quote.
Is disaster recovery only needed for natural disasters?
No. Disaster recovery plans commonly cover ransomware attacks, hardware failure, power outages, and human error, in addition to natural disasters like fire or flood. Cyber incidents are now among the most common triggers for DR activation.
